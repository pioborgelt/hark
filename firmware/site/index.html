Hark firmware for voice satellites

The stock firmware for your device with one extra setting: Wake word engine location. Flip it to In Home Assistant and the satellite streams its microphone to Home Assistant, so Hark can listen for any wake phrase you type. Left on On device, it behaves identically to the original firmware.

Your device
Test build (16 MB Voice PE only). This targets the standard 16 MB Voice PE — the 8 MB variant isn't supported yet. The streaming logic was tested end-to-end on a ReSpeaker Lite and is now being tried on real Voice PEs. Feedback is super welcome in issue #1, and you can flash back to stock over USB anytime (see below).

Install over USB (once)
  1. Connect the Voice PE to your computer with a USB-C data cable.
  2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on Windows, macOS or Linux (not a phone).
  3. Click the button, pick the device in the port list and follow the prompts. Flashing over USB wipes the device, so it will ask for your Wi-Fi at the end.
Your browser can't talk to USB devices. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer. This page needs to be opened over HTTPS.

After installing

  1. Set up the Hark app and an assistant with speech-to-text (see the Hark app documentation).
  2. Add the Voice PE in Home Assistant (Settings > Devices & services, "Discovered") if it isn't there yet.
  3. On its device page, set Assistant to the assistant using Hark. Satellites don't follow the "preferred" assistant.
  4. Set Wake word engine location to In Home Assistant.
  5. Say your wake word, pause a beat, and speak your command.

Need the full walkthrough or hit a snag? Check the ESP32 satellite guide. The center button, mute switch, timers and the "stop" word work just like stock; built-in wake words ("Okay Nabu", etc.) stay quiet in this mode. Streaming status and Voice phase under Diagnostic show what the device is doing.

This build doesn't include Bluetooth Wi-Fi provisioning. To change Wi-Fi later, plug it in over USB and use this page or web.esphome.io.

Updates

You only need USB once. New Hark builds show up as regular firmware updates right on the device page in Home Assistant — pulled from this page instead of upstream, so updating keeps the switch.

Going back to stock firmware

Flash the official firmware over USB using the Voice PE installer. Updates then come from Nabu Casa again.

Flash formatBCE's firmware over USB using the ReSpeaker Lite installer.
